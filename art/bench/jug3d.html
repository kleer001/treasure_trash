Bottom row, left to right: the raccoon facing right, the Blender square jug, the raccoon facing down, the game's round jug, the wheelie bin, a puddle, the bag. Above the raccoon facing down: the same jug as pixel art. The jug is rendered live at game pixels, camera straight on: a reference to draw over, not the sprite. Rotation applies Z, then Y, then X.
Height stretches the plain wall between the foot band and the taper band; the middle band keeps its size. Rib depth scales how far each band and groove stands out from the wall. Each pixel of plastic takes the no-water colour, darkened toward the deep-water colour by how much water you look through there; water darkness sets how fast. The plastic's light and shade stay on top.
The pixel jug takes the light in the jug's own frame, so it stays lit from the same side as the jug turns. Each pixel falls in a band of that light: shade, middle, light or shine. Where you see through at least the water depth, the band takes a water colour, one band darker past the deep depth; elsewhere it takes the plastic colours, and the middle band takes the empty fill, one of the eight colours below. Tidy gives a lone pixel the colour of its neighbours.
