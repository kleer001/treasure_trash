# The block-pusher shelf, and the free browser field

*Trash Panda* is a **thematic** neighbour to the raccoon-and-garbage idea: same animal, same
garbage, same fantasy. It is not a block-pusher, and its Mixed 40-review performance is not the
genre's bar. The bar is the Sokoban-family shelf, where the audience is small, extremely
literate about the genre, and unforgiving about level design.

All figures read 2026-08-18, from the Steam review API and storefront search.

Two facts to take from this table.

**One: this genre does not produce Mixed ratings.** Every entry above is Very or Overwhelmingly
Positive. Puzzle players either accept a game or ignore it; the ones who buy it are the ones who
already knew what they were buying. A weak puzzle game does not get a Mixed rating, it gets no
reviews at all.

**Two: the ceiling is real but the floor is thin.** Sokobond Express has 107 reviews with a
long-pedigree developer behind it. Below about $15 and outside the Draknek orbit, the tail falls
off fast. Nobody on this shelf got there on theme; they got there on the quality of the levels,
and several are famous specifically for level design.

## The irreversibility corner of the genre

Games whose central idea is that acting **permanently rewrites the board**, in decreasing order
of closeness to a purely additive obstacle mechanic:

- **A Monster's Expedition** (Draknek, 2020) — push trees, they fall and *become permanent
  bridges*. Terrain created by acting, which then has to be routed around and over. The nearest
  published relative to "your actions rewrite the floor", except its changes are usually
  *enabling* rather than *disabling*.
- **Bonfire Peaks** (Corey Martin / Draknek, 2021) — a 3D-grid block-pusher whose goal is to
  burn your own belongings. Consumption as a goal rather than an obstacle, but the same
  emotional register: the board is a thing you use up.
- **Classic Sokoban** (Thinking Rabbit, 1982) — the origin of irreversible deadlock. You cannot
  pull, so a wrong push is permanent. In Sokoban this is a failure mode rather than the subject.
- **Bomb-Sokoban variants** — the direct inverse. Bombs *clear* space rather than closing it.

**Nothing on this list is themed.** They are logs, crates, atoms, trains. The block-pusher shelf
is aggressively abstract, which is both the opening and the risk (see `the-gap.md`).

## The free browser field

itch.io, tags `puzzle` + `sokoban`: **2,297 results**. Sampling the front of that list, the
overwhelming majority are free, browser-playable, and jam-scale. Patrick's Parabox and Void
Stranger keep itch pages but are paid downloads; nearly everything free is browser HTML5.

Two consequences.

**Discovery is the binding constraint, not quality.** 2,297 competitors, most free, most
browser, most decent, is a field where a good game is invisible by default. Being better than
the median sokoban on itch is not a plan.

**Nothing in that field is wearing the raccoon costume.** In the sampled front rank there is no
raccoon and no trash theme — a pug-themed sokoban was the closest thing to a mascot. The
crossing of *proven-viral animal fantasy* × *block-pusher* has no occupant on either side: the
raccoon games have no puzzle, and the puzzles have no raccoon.

## What the crossing is worth, honestly

*Trash Panda* is proof that the fantasy generates press and goodwill **on its own**, from a
first-time developer with no games-industry contacts, twice — as a prototype in 2021 and at
launch in 2023. That is unusual, and it is the most transferable asset in this research.

It is not proof that the fantasy sells a puzzle game. What sold on the shelf above was level
design. The theme is the reason someone clicks; the rooms are the reason they stay.

## Where Treasure Trash goes on this shelf

Intent as of 2026-08-19, not a listing — see [`the-game.md`](./the-game.md). It is a
Sokoban-family block-pusher wearing the raccoon costume, which is the crossing this file just
measured as empty in both directions.

**On the free browser field:** it is already there, in the sense that matters — a page you open
with no install, which is what the whole 2,297 are. The plan is to keep that free and put a price
on the full pack, so the browser build is the demo rather than a thing to build later. That is
the cheap half of the Baba pattern taken as given rather than as a project.

**On the paid shelf:** the table above is the range to be honest inside. $4.99 buys 184 reviews
and $29.99 buys 1,580 — price on that shelf tracks how much game is behind it, not how the
storefront is dressed, and the fair time to name a number is when the rooms are all in. Around a
hundred of them is the current target, against 61 built.

**What it does not get for free:** everything above the theme. Being the only raccoon on the
shelf gets the click; nothing on this table got its rating any way but the rooms.
