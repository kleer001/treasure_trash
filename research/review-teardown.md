# Trash Panda — what its players actually said

Source: all 39 English Steam reviews, pulled 2026-08-18 (`raw/trashpanda-reviews.json`).
26 positive, 13 negative. Quotes verbatim, lightly trimmed for length only.

This is the most valuable artifact in this directory. It is a free, self-selected sample of the
exact audience the raccoon fantasy attracts, telling a developer in their own words what the
fantasy owes them and did not pay.

## The shape of the sample

| | |
|---|---|
| Reviews | 39 English (40 all-languages) |
| Median recorded playtime | **0.8 hours** |
| Under one hour | 21 of 39 |
| Longest sessions | 8.0h and 10.7h — both negative, both about grinding bins |
| Reviews long enough to argue (≥200 chars) | 14 — split exactly 7 positive / 7 negative |

The one-liners are almost all positive ("Raccoon.", "this is literally me.", "racoons are cool,
dude."). The considered reviews are split down the middle. **The costume bought the goodwill;
the game spent it in under an hour.**

Of the 14 substantive reviews, **7 use the word "potential"** and 4 explicitly ask for goals
or objectives. Nobody who used the word meant it kindly.

## The single finding

> "There's no actual game here at all, no explanation of what to do, no story, no meaningful
> goals. The only thing there is to do is to knock bins over and eat garbage. I know, that
> sounds great, but it gets old in about 30 seconds." — negative, 0.2h

> "Gameplay is very simple and not very expansive… I will stress that there is a lot of
> potential but I do not believe it is worth buying in its current state… it just needs some
> TLC (and some objectives/goals) to really capture people." — negative, 2.1h

> "Nice idea and I like the vibe, but sadly the game lacks any depth at all. The different maps
> don't really add much, and **the core mechanic is basically discovered and mastered within the
> first few minutes.** Then it's just grinding to tip over all the bins." — negative, 10.7h

> "More Potential than Game" — the title of the most-upvoted review in the set (51 helpful votes),
> which then spends several hundred words praising the physics and the animation.

> "Definitely add some missions or more activities also." — **positive**, 1.6h

> "It is a very good representation of what being a raccoon is, but after about 10 minutes it's
> rather boring." — negative, 3.4h

An audience asked, in writing, on the storefront, for a raccoon-and-garbage game with a defined
goal, a reason to choose one action over another, and a thing to be good at. It never arrived.

## What they praised — and this is the part to take seriously

The praise is almost never about content. It is about **the physical comedy of one verb.**

> "The physics of the individual pieces of trash is good, and probably one of the most
> entertaining things about this game… a raccoon yeeting a full sized herby curby across a
> street in a single slap is entertaining."

> "wonderful game. sastiates my urge to constantly knock over various trash bins within the
> vicinity."

> "This game is hilarious. Knocking over trashcans and running around Toronto being a Trash
> Panda is surprisingly satisfying."

> "The animation and art style, although both janky, is very charming."

> "Add chirp" — 4 helpful votes, on a four-word review

The verb — *strike the container, watch the contents scatter* — carried this game to Mixed on
its own, with no structure under it and a broken camera on top of it. The evidence says that
verb is worth animating and scoring for feel, not merely for correctness, in anything that
uses it.

**Observation (this research, not a finding of fact):** every unit of praise in this sample is
about the *moment* of the strike, and every complaint is about what surrounds it. A game that
put a real puzzle around that moment would be answering a demand already stated out loud — but
only if the strike itself landed with the same physical comedy. Rendered as a tile update rather
than an event, none of this praise transfers.

## Where the negatives do *not* apply

Roughly half the negative weight is on things a one-screen deterministic grid structurally
cannot have:
3D camera collision, cars spawning through roads, terrain that fails to load, invisible walls,
large empty maps with no fast travel, unobtainable achievements pointing at unbuilt levels.
A deterministic grid on one screen has no room for any of it.

Worth stating plainly rather than reading as a compliment to any alternative: these are
3D-open-world failures, so **avoiding them is a consequence of choosing a different format, not
an achievement.** The axis that actually matters is the one the negatives all share — is there
anything here to be good at — and on that axis the bar sits on the shelf in
`competitive-landscape.md`, not here.

## The price lesson

Six reviews mention money; five of those negatively, and the recurring verdict is "get it on
sale" or "you are simply donating". $9.99 set an expectation of *a game*, and what shipped was
an experience. A free browser game makes no such promise and can be tried in the time it takes to read a
review — which is also why it has to land its hook in the first thirty seconds rather than the
first thirty minutes.
