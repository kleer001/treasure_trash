# Trash Panda — where the attention actually came from

Channel-by-channel forensics. The question is not "did it get attention" — it got two waves of
it — but **which channel produced it, whether that channel was repeatable, and whether the
people it reached were people who buy games.**

Figures read 2026-08-18.

## The short answer

**Local broadcast news.** Not Reddit, not YouTube, not a games outlet, not word of mouth among
players. A TV segment lit it, city-interest blogs relayed it, and nothing else ever moved.

## The channels, one at a time

### Television — the only thing that worked

The May 2021 viral moment traces to a single cause. blogTO's own piece on it says: *"thousands
have shown interest in the game since it was featured by CTV on Monday."* The prototype did not
climb; it was picked up.

CBC ran a video segment. CTV News Toronto ran a piece. CFRB ran a radio interview (a player
mentions it on the Steam board). This is the wave that turned a hobby into a project, and the
developer has said as much.

**Why it is worth naming precisely:** a TV producer chose this story because it was about
*Toronto and raccoons*, not because it was a good game. That is a channel you cannot plan, cannot
repeat, and cannot aim — and it delivers an audience of local news viewers, most of whom do not
buy $10 indie games on Steam.

### Blogs — relay, not discovery

blogTO (twice: May 2021, Nov 2023), Daily Hive Toronto (twice), curiocity, Secret Toronto,
Toast Life, Exclaim!, MobileSyrup, mxdwn Games. Every one of these is a city-interest or
lifestyle outlet running the same story with the same angle.

Games press proper: **essentially none.** Kotaku holds a database entry with no article. No
review at a games outlet, no Metacritic critic score.

### Reddit — did not happen

Searched r/toronto for the game, all time, top-sorted: **one relevant post**, a link to the
blogTO article, **41 votes and 8 comments**, from 2021. A whole-of-Reddit search for the game
returns nothing on topic at all.

For a game whose entire premise is a running local joke in a city with a 498,000-member
subreddit, this is the striking number in the whole file. The story was told *at* Torontonians
by television; it was never taken up *by* them.

The top two are news organisations doing news. **Every video made by an actual games creator is
in the double or low-triple digits.** No streamer, no let's-player, no critic, no analysis
video, at any size, ever. For a game whose selling point is physical comedy — the exact material
that normally travels on YouTube — that is a total miss.

### The channels the developer worked, and what they returned

| Channel | What was done | Visible result |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Next Fest | Demo + developer stream, Feb 2022 | Demo videos at 15–218 views |
| "Games from Ontario" Steam event | Four announcements, Sept 2022 | none traceable |
| Discord | Community server, running from 2021 | not measurable from outside |
| Launch party | In person, Toronto Metropolitan University, launch day | one board post about it |
| Brand collab | Goodfood, No Food Waste Day, April 2024 — in-game skin + a porch-pirate mechanic, Canada only | partner's own video: 335 views |
| Steam announcements | 18 posts, Sept 2022 – June 2024 | — |

The Goodfood collab is the most ambitious thing on this list, and it is the clearest data point:
a national meal-kit company put its mascot in the game, and the video it made about it was seen
335 times.

### Word of mouth — the thing that never started

40 Steam reviews in three years. Median playtime 0.8 hours. One concurrent player at the read
date. Whatever the news coverage delivered, it did not compound.

## The pattern

Every channel that worked was **push**: somebody with a broadcast platform decided to tell
people about it. Nothing **pull** ever engaged — no community adopted it, no creator made a
video about it because they wanted to, no player recommended it hard enough to move a number.

The distinction matters because push channels do not stack. A TV segment reaches its audience
once and is gone. Pull channels compound for years, which is the entire mechanism behind the two
games in the next two files.

**Observation (this research):** the fantasy earned the push and the game could not convert it
to pull. Reviewers named the reason in writing — nothing to do — and the review teardown has
them saying it. Anything entering this space inherits the problem in reverse: no TV producer
runs a segment about a browser puzzle game, so **pull is the only channel on offer**, and it has
to be engineered rather than hoped for.
