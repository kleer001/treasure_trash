# Stephen's Sausage Roll — the peer-endorsement playbook

The control case: a block-pusher with **no marketing at all** that outsold Trash Panda by an
order of magnitude at three times the price, and is still selling a decade later.

Figures read 2026-08-18.

## The facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Developer | Stephen Lavelle (increpare), solo |
| Released | 18 April 2016 |
| Price | $29.99 USD (CDN$38.99) — never a budget title |
| Platforms | Windows, macOS, Linux |
| Steam reviews | **1,580, Overwhelmingly Positive** — and 14 in the recent window, ten years on |
| SteamSpy owners | 100,000 – 200,000 |
| Metacritic | **90/100** from 9 critics |
| Notable scores | Destructoid 10/10, The Guardian 5/5, Edge 9/10, Game Informer 8/10 |
| Award | Edge PC Game of the Year, 2017 |

For scale against the raccoon simulator studied here: **39× the reviews, ~10× the owners, 3×
the price, and a critic score where Trash Panda has none.**

## The store page, in full

The entire Steam description reads:

> **A simple 3d puzzle game.**

Six words. No feature bullets, no pillars, no adjectives, no pitch. The game shipped with less
marketing copy than most games put in a tweet, and it is Overwhelmingly Positive with 1,580
reviews.

**The lesson is not "write less."** It is that copy was not the variable. What sold this game
happened entirely outside the store page.

## What actually sold it: two developers vouching

Before release, **Jonathan Blow** (Braid, The Witness) and **Bennett Foddy** (QWOP, Getting Over
It) both praised it publicly. Blow's line is the one that travelled:

> "one of the best puzzle games ever made, or one of the best video games ever made"

and

> "After this game comes out, if your game design school isn't teaching it, then it is just a
> bad school."

Foddy compared its difficulty to Dark Souls.

That is the whole launch campaign. Two people the target audience already trusted said it was
important, and the target audience — puzzle players, who are a small, dense, extremely
well-connected group — moved. A YouTube let's-play in the search results is literally titled
*"Puzzle Game Recommended by Jonathan Blow!"*

Three things in that table.

**A critic's essay outperformed the developer's own trailer 2.6 to 1.** The single largest piece
of marketing this game ever received was made by someone else, for free, about why it is good.

**Game Grumps found it seven years after launch** and brought it 600k+ views in two videos.
There was no launch window to miss.

**Someone published a new state-space analysis of the opening levels within the past year**, and
64,000 people watched it. A decade on, the game is still generating explanation.

## The long tail is the whole business

14 recent Steam reviews, ten years after release, on a $30 game that has never been marketed.
Owners in the 100–200k band accumulated slowly and are still accumulating.

Compare the incumbent: 40 reviews, one concurrent player, dev gone since 2024. Trash Panda had
its whole life in its launch month. SSR has had ten years and is not finished.

## Why the price is part of the story

$29.99 for a 2016 indie puzzle game was conspicuous, and it was noticed. It functioned as a
claim about seriousness — this is not a snack — and the reviews and critics validated the claim
rather than punishing it. The community's joke Steam tags ("Psychological Horror", "Cooking")
are the sound of an audience that adopted the game as an in-group object.

Contrast the price finding in `review-teardown.md`: $9.99 for Trash Panda read as *too much for
what this is*, and five of six money-mentioning reviews said so. **Price is not high or low, it
is congruent or incongruent with what the thing turns out to be.**

## What transfers

- **One credible peer endorsement is worth more than every city-interest blog in Canada.** The
  puzzle audience is small and takes recommendations from a handful of known names.
- **Make the game explainable.** The top video for this game is an essay about why it works.
  A mechanic that rewards analysis recruits analysts, and analysts have audiences.
- **There is no launch window.** Seven years after release a large channel picked it up cold.
  Nothing about a good puzzle game expires.
- **Store copy is not the lever.** Six words did fine.
