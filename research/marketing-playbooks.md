# Three playbooks, side by side

The lay of the land. One thematic competitor and two genre exemplars, compared on the only
question that matters: **what caused the attention, and did it compound?**

Figures read 2026-08-18. Details and sources in the three case files.

## What lit each one

**Trash Panda — local broadcast news.** A CTV segment in May 2021 produced the viral moment;
CBC, CTV, blogTO, Daily Hive and city-lifestyle blogs relayed it. Reddit never engaged (one
r/toronto post, 41 votes). No games creator ever made a video. **Push, once, at an audience that
was not a games audience.**

**Stephen's Sausage Roll — two developers vouching.** Jonathan Blow and Bennett Foddy praised it
publicly before release. The store description is six words long. **Pull, from inside the genre,
compounding for a decade.**

**Baba Is You — a free build and a festival circuit.** Won Nordic Game Jam 2017, posted the jam
build free on itch, won two IGF awards on it a year before release, got a Nintendo Nindies
Showcase slot, then shipped. **Pull, validated by institutions, compounding for seven years.**

## The four patterns that repeat

### 1. In this genre, the top video is an essay

Baba's most-watched video is Game Maker's Toolkit explaining the design (1.9m). SSR's is Joseph
Anderson explaining the design (625k) — 2.6× the developer's own trailer. Trash Panda's is a
newspaper's news segment.

Puzzle games are marketed by people explaining them. That means **legibility is a growth
mechanic**: a rule you can state in one sentence and demonstrate in a five-second GIF recruits
critics, and critics have audiences that a developer cannot buy.

### 2. There is no launch window

Game Grumps picked up SSR **seven years** after release and delivered 600k+ views. SSR still
collects Steam reviews at ten years. Baba's itch page is still rated and still updated in 2026.

Trash Panda had its entire life in one month, twice, because news is an event and not a
relationship.

**Consequence:** a puzzle game that is good is never late. Nothing about the release date is
urgent, and effort spent on a launch spike is misallocated.

### 3. The free public artifact is the marketing

Baba's jam build was free, permanent, and won the awards that made everything after it possible.
It was not a funnel, a wishlist driver, or a cut-down demo — it was the idea, given away, where
anyone including festival judges could reach it.

This is the pattern most directly available to a free browser game, which is *already* that
artifact. The gap is not the build; it is that nobody has been pointed at it.

### 4. Presence compounds, and so does absence

Baba: devlogs through 2026, level editor, merch, vinyl, seven years of visible life.
Trash Panda: silent since June 2024, eighteen board threads, **zero developer replies on any of
them**, one titled "Abandoned by dev?".

The audience each one has today is the sum of those decisions.

## The channels this evidence actually names

Every one of these is verifiable and has a door that does not check whether anyone has heard of
you. Counts move — re-check before acting.

- **ThinkyGames.com and its Discord** (~9.4k members at the read date) — the genre's actual
  gathering place, with a curated games database and sokoban lists. This is where the people who
  played the two exemplars are.
- **The Thinky Puzzle Jam** — an annual itch.io jam run out of that community.
- **Thinky Third Thursday** — a recurring showcase for thinky games.
- **IGF and comparable festivals** — took a free jam build seriously and gave it two awards. A
  submission, not an introduction.
- **Analysis creators** — Game Maker's Toolkit, Joseph Anderson, Aliensrock, Snoman Gaming and
  their successors. They cover mechanics, not marketing budgets — and the discipline that pays
  before pitching one is to read the feed: what each has actually published recently, and what
  recurring format their last twenty items imply.
- **itch.io** — the free, browser-playable storefront the whole field already lives on.

And the one this evidence rules out: **local press.** The Toronto angle belongs to somebody
else's game, television does not run segments about browser puzzle games, and the audience it
delivers does not buy indie puzzle games. Copying that channel means copying the part of the
story that did not work.

## The single sentence

Trash Panda was *told about* by broadcasters and never adopted by anyone; the two exemplars were
adopted by a small, dense, opinionated genre community and then spent years compounding — so the
transferable move is not to seek coverage, it is to **put a free, finished, legible thing in
front of the people who argue about puzzle design, and then still be there in three years.**
