# Baba Is You — the free-build-plus-festivals playbook

The ceiling case, and the most directly instructive of the three: it began as a **free build
given away in public two years before anyone could buy it.**

Figures read 2026-08-18.

## The facts

| | |
|---|---|
| Developer | Arvi Teikari (Hempuli), Finland, solo, a student at the time |
| Origin | **Nordic Game Jam 2017**, theme "Not There" — won first place |
| Released | 13 March 2019 (Windows, macOS, Linux, Switch); mobile June 2021 |
| Price | $14.99 |
| Steam reviews | **24,473, Overwhelmingly Positive** |
| SteamSpy owners | **1,000,000 – 2,000,000**; 269 concurrent at the read date |
| Metacritic | 87 PC / 84 Switch; OpenCritic 89% recommended |
| itch.io | $14.99, **4.8/5 from 1,199 ratings**, devlogs running Dec 2022 → Feb 2026 |
| Jam build on itch | **free, still up**, 4.9/5 from 211 ratings, 2.7 MB |

## The two-year runway

This is the part worth copying, and it is a sequence, not a trick.

| When | What | What it cost |
|---|---|---|
| **2017** | Built at Nordic Game Jam, **won first place** | a weekend |
| **2017** | Jam build posted **free on itch.io**, publicly, permanently | a zip upload |
| **March 2018** | **IGF: won Best Student Game and Excellence in Design; nominated for the Seumas McNally Grand Prize** — all on the free jam build, a year before release | a festival submission |
| **Aug 2018** | Featured in **Nintendo's Nindies Showcase** — a platform holder putting it on their own channel | Nintendo's decision, earned by the above |
| **March 2019** | Release, PC + Switch, $14.99 | — |
| 2019–20 | The Game Awards Best Independent nominee; **DICE 2020 Outstanding Achievement in Game Design**; **GDC Choice Awards 2020 Best Design + Innovation**; BAFTA nominations | — |
| Nov 2021 | Level editor + 250 new levels | — |
| → 2026 | Devlogs, Fangamer merch, vinyl OST, still shipping versions | ongoing |

**The free build was the marketing.** It was not a demo carved out of a finished game to drive
wishlists; it was the whole idea, given away, two years early, where anyone could play it and
anyone could judge it. Every award above was won by something nobody had paid for.

The top result is **a design-analysis video, not gameplay and not the trailer** — the identical
shape as Stephen's Sausage Roll. Two entries are ordinary players posting a clip of something
funny the rules let them do. The developer's own soundtrack uploads clear 400k each.

**Why the rules-as-blocks design is a marketing asset:** the mechanic is *legible in a
screenshot and astonishing in a GIF*. A player who breaks it in a funny way has something to
post, an essayist has something to explain, and both of them market the game for free, forever.
That property is designed in, not bolted on.

## What the developer keeps doing

Devlogs on itch through February 2026. A level editor that turned players into content
producers. Merch through Fangamer, a vinyl soundtrack. Seven years after release, the itch page
is alive and the rating is 4.8 across 1,199 votes.

Set beside Trash Panda's last update in June 2024 and eight unanswered threads, this is the
other half of the same lesson: **presence compounds and absence compounds.**

## What transfers

- **Give the real thing away early, publicly, and permanently.** The free jam build won the
  awards that made the paid release possible. A free browser game is *already* this artifact —
  the question is whether it is put where judges and players can find it.
- **Festivals and jams are a channel with a submission fee and no gatekeeper.** IGF took a free
  jam build seriously. That door does not check whether anyone has heard of you.
- **Design for the clip and the essay.** The mechanic should produce moments a player wants to
  post and a critic wants to explain. That is what recruited 1.9m views for someone else's video.
- **Never stop being present.** Seven years of devlogs is the difference between a catalogue
  entry and a living game.

## The honest caveat

Baba Is You is a generational outlier — an original mechanic that had not existed before, from a
developer who won a major jam on the first try. Nothing here is a formula that reproduces the
outcome. What reproduces is the **order of operations**: free build in public → festival
validation → platform attention → paid release → permanent presence. That order is available to
anyone. The result is not.
