What to take from Lolo

A catalogue of every enemy, tile and object in HAL's Eggerland / Adventures of Lolo line, scored against what Treasure Trash can actually accept.

Research note. Nothing here is decided or implemented — the shipped ruleset is levels.md and src/rules.mjs. Every Lolo behaviour below is quoted from the sources listed at the foot of the page; every claim about Treasure Trash is from its own code.

The short version: take Lolo's floor, leave Lolo's monsters. Everything people remember about Lolo — Medusa, the Skulls, Alma curling into a ball — is real‑time and lethal, and Treasure Trash is neither. Everything under their feet is static, deterministic and full‑information, which is exactly what this game already is. The split is a claim about the rules, not about the engine — the engine will hold either kind cheaply. See what a new piece costs.

Lolo's shape and ours already rhyme, which is why the comparison is worth making at all. Lolo clears every Heart Framer to open the Jewel Box, then leaves. The raccoon tears every bag, then stands on the exit. Same contract — finish the objective, then get out — and the README already names Lolo as lineage.

The difference is the sign. Lolo's objective removes things from the board; ours adds them. A collected Heart Framer is gone and the room gets roomier. A torn bag sprays five permanent cells of trash and the room gets tighter. That inversion is the whole reason a Lolo element can look like a perfect fit and still be unusable: half of Lolo's catalogue is about clearing space, and levels.md states the pillar flatly — only add or relocate, never remove, and there is no chute and no disposal object anywhere in the game.

What a new piece actually costs. Less than it looks, in both directions — and it is worth being exact, because a wrong cost estimate quietly re-ranks everything below it.

An occupant costs a code, and codes are not scarce. Nine are defined (NONE, BAG, CAN_FULL, CAN_EMPTY, TRASH, BIN, STACK, WHEELIE, WHEELIE_EMPTY) and stateKey gives each one printable character, so there is room for scores more. The key is opaque — nothing parses it, and solver.mjs only ever uses it as a Map key. Whether a piece should be an occupant is a question about the piece, not about the budget.

Terrain costs less still, and for a reason that does not go away. The key is occupants + raccoon position; walls, water and the exit flag never enter it. Static terrain constrains a room without growing the state graph the solver walks — and that solver is exhaustive, so every extra bit of state is paid for in every room, forever. That is a real asymmetry rather than an accounting artifact.

So: prefer terrain when a piece is genuinely static, because the solver is the thing you are spending. Do not prefer it merely to avoid defining a code.

1Terrain & tiles

The richest category, and the cheapest. Lolo's floor is a genuine design language — seven distinct tiles, each defined by a small table of who may pass and what may pass through. Treasure Trash currently has three: floor, wall, exit.

TakeArrow TileOne-Way Pass Forces a route to be taken in one order rather than any order. Lolo may enter from any side but “cannot pass over the Arrow mark in reverse direction”. Monsters ignore it entirely; Emerald Framers and eggs obey it. Its direction can be rotated with a Power item. The curb. A cell you can drop off but not climb back onto — cans included. In a game with no pull, a one-way tile is the cleanest instrument there is for forcing order, and it answers an open sketch in levels.md by name: “a bag that must be opened last.” Today nothing can force that outright. High Very
TakeTree vs. Rocktwo walls, one permeable Two blockers that look different and are different — the distinction is what passes through, not who walks on. Both stop Lolo and every monster. Medusa's gaze “can pass through trees” but not rocks; Magic Shots pass through neither. Rocks can be destroyed with the Hammer. Chain-link fence vs. brick. The fence stops the raccoon but accepts fan trash — debris hits it and sticks. Brick refuses it, as walls do now. This is the highest-leverage item on the page: levels.md records that a fencing room on a wide board “has to be built out of trash the player has already laid down, not out of level geometry”, because a fan cell landing on a wall refuses the whole strike. A trash-permeable wall gives that room type back. Very high Very
ShippedEgg as raftthe additive half of water Turns an obstacle into a crossing — the one place in Lolo where a push opens the board instead of closing it. Push an egg into water and it floats; Lolo rides it across and steps off where he needs to. “Sometimes the egg will just sink,” so placement is the puzzle. Emerald Framers can never be pushed onto water. The puddle you fill — shipped as water. A cell the raccoon won't cross until something is dumped in it. The shipped rule is narrower than the raft and better for it: water refuses cans exactly as the exit does, and accepts only trash, laid by a bag's fan or dropped by the recycle bin. Trash therefore means blocked on dry ground and walkable on water — the one place where making a mess buys you something. Terrain, so it never enters the state key. Done Very
MaybeBreak TileLolo 3 Makes walking a limited resource — the only tile that does. A bridge tile that “can only be used twice”: it cracks after the first two crossings, then shatters and is gone. Introduced in Lolo 3 (earlier in the Japanese release). The rotten pallet. Same rule, visible crack count. Strongly on-pillar — the board only ever gets worse. The one rule it negotiates with: verify.mjs enforces that a plain move must never take a winnable board to a dead one. A break tile makes walking spend something. Lolo's own answer is that the tile visibly cracks, so take that version and relax the assertion to no unsignposted traps. Medium High
MaybeFlower Bed A cell only the player may enter — a safe island inside a hostile room. “Lolo can move through this tile but no monster can.” Gazes and shots pass over it; Framers and eggs can be pushed onto it. As written it's dead on arrival — it filters monsters, and the crow is pinned. Its inversion is the useful one: a grating the raccoon walks over but no object can be pushed across. A soft wall that constrains pushes only, at zero state cost. Park the original with the crow. High Medium
MaybeDesert Makes distance cost something, so the short route and the cheap route can differ. “Lolo will move at half-speed through these tiles while monsters will move normally.” Everything else passes normally. Gravel or soaked cardboard: a cell that costs two moves. It's a real dial — it would make par a cost rather than a step count. But it breaks two things at once: par is currently the LURD token count (FORMATS.md), and the solver is a uniform-cost BFS that would have to become Dijkstra. Buys a difficulty knob the ladder already has by other means. Low-med Medium
NoWater & Lava Hard negative space, and a way to make objects disappear. Neither Lolo nor any monster may enter. Gazes and shots pass over both. Framers can't be pushed onto either; eggs float on water only. Bridges span both, but a bridge over lava “will last for approximately 5 seconds before they burn up.” Ruled out by the pillar, not by the engineering. A tile that swallows a can is a disposal object, and levels.md is explicit that nothing on the board ever removes anything. The burning bridge additionally needs a clock, and this game is untimed. The salvageable half is the raft, one row up. — —

2Objects & items

Mostly a scorecard rather than a shopping list: Lolo's object set and ours already converged, independently. The items that haven't been imported turn out to be blocked by one decision each — and it's the same decision three times, that collecting was cut.

ElementPurpose in LoloBehaviourSuggested translation Conf.Awesome
ShippedHeart Framer The objective. Collect every one to finish the room. Static, unpushable, and a shield: “Heart Framers will protect Lolo from all attacks, but if he steps on it he no longer has that protection.” They also power the Magic Shots. Already the garbage bag — with the sign flipped. Lolo's hearts leave the board; the raccoon's bags become five more obstacles. Same objective shape, opposite effect on space, and that inversion is the game. Done Core
ShippedEmerald Framer The pushable block — the Sokoban primitive. “A movable shield… indestructible, and only one can be pushed at a time. If two blocks are touching, they cannot be moved together in the same direction.” Already CAN_EMPTY, under the same law. Worth noting that Lolo arrived at the identical rule — one block, never two — which is quiet evidence the empty can is shaped right. Done Core
ShippedJewel Box The way out, and the reason to finish the objective before leaving. Sealed until the last Heart Framer is taken. “Lolo can walk through it, while other enemies cannot” — but it is no protection from Medusa, Don Medusa or Gol. Already the exit, and ours is the stricter version: the engine refuses any strike or push that would land trash, a can or a bag on it, so it can never be sealed. Lolo's box can't be buried either — same instinct, enforced differently. Done Core
NoEgg Converts a threat into a tool — the series' signature verb. A Magic Shot turns a monster into a pushable egg that “will revert back to a Monster after some time.” A second shot sends it off-screen to reappear later at its original position. Eggs block gazes, trap monsters and float. The conversion half is already the core loop, pointed the other way: bag becomes trash. The reversion half is a timer, and the game is untimed by policy. Nothing left to import except the raft, which is listed under terrain. Low Low
NoPower ItemsBridge · Hammer · Arrow Changer Consumable verbs, bought with the objective you were collecting anyway. Bridge spans one tile of water or lava. Hammer destroys an adjacent rock. Arrow Changer rotates an adjacent arrow tile 90° clockwise. All are powered by Heart Framers. All three need a charge economy, which needs collecting, which levels.md lists under Cut. The Hammer removes a rock and fails the pillar twice over. The Arrow Changer is the only one worth revisiting, and only as a terrain rule rather than ammunition, and only if the curb lands first. Low Low-med
NoSpecial Heart Framer A hidden reward — indistinguishable from the ordinary kind until claimed. Looks exactly like a normal Heart Framer; grants “two Magic Shots” when taken. Rejected on policy, not on cost. This is hidden information, and the fan preview is on in every room because “a full-information puzzle owes the player that answer forever.” A tile that lies about what it is contradicts the contract the whole game is built on. — —

3Enemies

Eight monsters, and on the face of it none of them fit: they move in real time, they kill on contact, and this game has one animal in it. But two are worth taking anyway, because their rule survives when the creature is thrown away — and a third turns out to describe a piece that already shipped.

ElementPurpose in LoloBehaviourSuggested translation Conf.Awesome
TakeGol sprite Puts a sting in the walk home — the room changes character the moment you finish it. “Gols… remain stationary facing one direction. When Lolo collects all of the Heart Framers in a room, they wake up and shoot a fireball if he walks into their line of sight.” One shot at a time; it can't fire again until the first is gone. The gate that shuts when the alley is wrecked. Drop the fireball, keep the trigger: terrain that changes state the instant the last bag is torn. This attacks a real weakness — the authoring checklist demands the exit forbid at least one action precisely because the walk to it otherwise degenerates into “a walk-back tax”. And it is free: every bag torn is already bagsLeft(s) === 0, derived from occupants, so it adds nothing to the state key. High Very
TakeMedusa sprite Denies territory from a distance, and lets the player rebuild what's denied by moving blocks into the line. Stationary. “It can shoot Lolo with its petrifying stare in all four directions (even through trees!), but is incapable of doing so through rocks, Heart Framers, Emerald Framers, eggs, stationary monsters, and even mobile monsters.” Only the cell directly in front is fatal; it cannot be egged or shot away. The motion light, or a dog on a chain. Strip the death and the clock and what's left is static and deterministic: a cell that projects four lines of denial until something blocks them, recomputed after every push. The player reshapes the lit region by shoving cans into it. The one genuine cost is analytical, not structural: the trap checker has to learn about cells that are legal to stand on only sometimes, which is a category it does not have today. High Very
MaybeMobyLolo 3, underwater The only monster that moves you rather than blocking you. A whale in the underwater rooms; cross its line of sight and it sucks Lolo in towards it. Not directly fatal, but it can strand him and force a restart. The manual calls it “an underwater Snakey, with one major difference.” The storm drain. A cell that drags the raccoon along its line until he fetches up against something — the wheelie bin's rule, turned on the player. It's the one Lolo idea that adds a movement verb instead of another blocker. Note that no pull governs the raccoon pulling objects; terrain moving the raccoon is a different rule. Needs the same signposting the break tile does, for the same reason. Medium High
ShippedAlma sprite A chaser with a commitment problem you can exploit. Chases Lolo, fatal on contact — but “if an Alma is aligned with Lolo horizontally, it will roll into a ball and keep moving in that direction… the ball will keep rolling until it hits something.” The wheelie bin already is this. Roll-until-you-hit-something shipped in L6 at par 11. No work to do here — but it's worth knowing that the same rule carried a marquee enemy in 1989, and that the reason it's expensive for us (the bin dumps its bag adjacent to itself, so every container costs two relocations) is our own adjacency tax, not the rule's fault. Done High
ParkedLeeper sprite Lets the player convert a moving nuisance into permanent furniture, for better or worse. Roams freely. “Once Lolo touches one, it will fall asleep and remain in that position permanently (which is possibly good or bad, either blocking another enemy or perhaps trapping Lolo). Once a Leeper is asleep, it can no longer be put into an egg.” Touch-to-freeze is exactly this game's pillar — maximum mess, nothing gets cleaned up, and the choice of where to freeze it is the puzzle. There is simply nothing mobile on the board to freeze. Parked with the crow: if the crow ever unpins, this is the rule that makes its leavings permanent. Blocked High later
NoSnakey sprite Scenery that's in the way, and raw material for an egg. “Completely stationary and pose no real threat to Lolo except that they may get in his way from time to time.” Can be egged and then pushed, or floated as a raft. A stationary obstacle that becomes pushable is the empty can with an extra step in front of it. Nothing here that CAN_EMPTY doesn't already do. High Low
NoSkull sprite Turns the last stretch into a chase — the endgame pressure valve. “Remain dormant until all of the Heart Framers within a room are collected, at which time they become active and prowl throughout the area… it is necessary to find a way to confine the Skulls before all of the heart frames are collected.” Needs a real-time actor and makes plain moves lethal — two direct violations. The trigger is the good part, and it's the same trigger as Gol; take it there, without the chase. Low Low-med
NoDon Medusa sprite A moving denial line — timing and position at once. “Don Medusas only move back and forth either vertically or horizontally… once Lolo is within their line of sight, they will throw a sword which is instantly fatal.” Cannot be egged; lethal to touch. A fixed patrol is deterministic — one step per player move would work. But it multiplies the state graph by its period, and the solver is exhaustive. It also makes plain moves lethal. Expensive and off-pillar in the same breath. Low Medium
NoRocky sprite A slow blocker that suddenly isn't slow. “Walks at half of Lolo's speed… if they are aligned with Lolo vertically they will charge him at full speed… When a Rocky is in close proximity to Lolo it will stop moving in an attempt to block his path.” Every interesting thing about Rocky is a speed differential, and there is no clock to differentiate against. The align-and-charge rule is Alma's, already shipped as the wheelie bin. Low Low

4The four to build

Ranked by what they unblock, not by how good they sound, and not by which half of the engine they land in — three are terrain and one is an occupant, which is the right mix rather than a concession.

  1. The chain-link fence — a wall that stops the raccoon but accepts fan trash.

    Picked first because it's the only one that unblocks a room type the design doc says can't currently be built: a wide board can't fence you in with level geometry today, because a fan cell landing on a wall refuses the strike outright. One terrain flag, one branch in the fan-blocker check.

  2. The gate that shuts when the last bag tears — Gol's trigger, no fireball.

    Free. bagsLeft(s) === 0 is already derived from the occupant grid, so nothing joins the state key and the solver doesn't grow. Makes the walk to the exit a puzzle instead of a victory lap — which the authoring checklist already had to legislate around.

  3. The curb — a one-way cell, for the raccoon and for anything pushed.

    The cleanest way to force order in a game with no pull, and the direct answer to the open sketch “a bag that must be opened last.” Wants one real refactor: isOccupiable() takes only (x, y) today and would need to know which way something is entering from.

  4. The motion light — Medusa's denial lines, without Medusa.

    The one occupant on the list, and the only pick that adds a constraint the player can reshape rather than merely route around: shove a can into the beam and the lit region changes. Last of the four because it is the only one that asks something new of the analysis — a cell that is legal to stand on only sometimes is a category the trap checker doesn't have. Build it when that checker is worth extending, not before.

Already built: the puddle. Water is in, as terrain rather than as an occupant — and with a sharper rule than the obvious one. It refuses cans exactly the way the exit does; the only thing it accepts is trash, laid by a bag's fan or dropped in by the recycle bin. So trash reads as blocked on dry ground and walkable on water, which makes it the one place in the game where making a mess buys you something rather than costing you something. That is a better idea than the raft it came from.

And one flattering thing the research turned up. Alma's roll-until-you-hit-something carried a marquee enemy in 1989, and it's the wheelie bin, shipped in L6. Lolo's Emerald Framer rule — one block at a time, never two touching — is the empty can's rule, arrived at independently. The convergence isn't a coincidence so much as a sign the grammar was already right. What Lolo still has more of is a floor that participates — seven tiles that each answer who may pass, and what may pass through. Water is the first cell here to answer that question at all, and the rest of this page is mostly arguing for more of them.
